Our Approach

The human foundations of governance

We study how order takes shape within a person, between people, and how it endures over time.

MNERVA recognizes that durable governance depends not only on rules and incentives, but also on people’s capacity to remain present, regulate themselves, and stay in relationship under stress.

Within a person

Attention, self-regulation, and body awareness shape how a person responds to uncertainty, exercises judgment, and remains present under pressure.

Between people

Roles, decision rights, and shared agreements give people ways to negotiate differences, distribute power, and remain in relationship through conflict.

Across generations

Succession requires both structures that can outlast their founders and people prepared to carry responsibility, adapt those structures, and make them their own.

Our Guiding Question

How do durable human systems make decisions, distribute power, resolve conflict, and survive their founders?

Structures and people

A governance system lives through the people who use it. We bring questions about formal structures into conversation with attention, self-regulation, and relationships.

Our work considers both the agreements that organize a group and the capacities people need to uphold, question, and renew them over time.
